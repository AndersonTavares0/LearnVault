## Product constraints (with sources)

LearnVault indexes, in read-only mode, course folders that belong to the user, keeps a catalog tree and only scans when the user explicitly asks; a new scan marks missing media without deleting records or study progress. Study progress includes a resume position, completion independent of position and recent history. Desktop mode is for a single user and works offline, with folder selection through a native picker and no login/network ports; server mode has a local-account login. The study interface includes course/lesson navigation, playback, resume, empty/error states, focus/Pomodoro, statistics and settings as product features to keep. These are product requirements, not references to the legacy interface: [requirements FR-01 to FR-10, NFR-01 to NFR-09](../product/requirements.md#functional-requirements), [roadmap](../product/roadmap.md). The requirements also demand keyboard navigation, visible focus, contrast, reduced motion and screen-reader labels [NFR-08](../product/requirements.md#non-functional-requirements). Do not use existing LearnVault screens, old design files or earlier prototypes as a model for the new visual direction.

### Functional inventory

- Catalog course folders that belong to the user in read-only mode, organized in a category → course → module → lesson tree; scanning only happens on explicit request.
- Preserve records and progress when media is missing after a new scan; distinguish this from explicit media removal.
- Offer playback and resume by position, with completion independent of position and recent history.
- Keep course/lesson navigation, empty and error states, focus/Pomodoro, statistics and settings.
- On the desktop, offer offline use for a single user and folder selection through a native picker, with no account required; on the server, offer a local-account login.
- Meet the requirements for keyboard navigation, visible focus, contrast, reduced motion and screen-reader labels.

## Synthesis for LearnVault

These are design directions derived from the cited product requirements and from the patterns in the references, not claims that a specific interface has been tested by users.


### Patterns observed in course platforms

Direct reading of public Udemy and Coursera pages reinforces a course-centred experience: an identifiable cover and legible metadata at discovery; instructor/goal and summary before the curriculum; content grouped by modules, with duration and progress when that data exists; next items coherent with the active course. For LearnVault, these shapes must represent the local library of the person studying, not a marketplace: no price, purchase, enrollment, invented ratings/students, certificates or synchronization. The visual identity can be richer and more welcoming than the G2 tabular index, as long as the covers do not hide search, state, progress or availability. Duration, materials and status must come from real data or be clearly demonstrative in the prototype.

### Navigation and discovery

Keep the contexts of the library/catalog and of the active course/lesson legible, with persistent orientation and a direct path back to the catalog. A collapsible desktop sidebar is a reasonable option to explore (Apple [Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)); keyboard navigation must complement the visible pointer controls, not replace them (Raycast [Navigation](https://manual.raycast.com/navigation); W3C [keyboard interface](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/)). Organize discovery according to the real category → course → module → lesson tree and present material that belongs to the user without suggesting downloads, enrollment or cloud synchronization ([RF-01–04](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais)). Search/filters/sorting can help find items in the collection, but must faithfully reflect the indexed metadata; Spotify documents library filters/sorting as behavior of its own product, not as a prescription for LearnVault ([Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/sort-and-filter)).

Make the state and consequences of scanning explicit: it only happens by user action; describe new, known and missing media without suggesting that the originals are moved or removed, and never indicate that a new scan deletes progress ([RF-01, RF-04](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais)). Course cards or rows must prioritize identifiable course/lesson information over decorative art; galleries such as Mobbin, Refero and Awwwards may inform only the composition, not these behavioral guarantees.

### Learning progress

In course/lesson views, show hierarchy, current lesson, previous/resume position and completion as separate states. Completion is explicitly independent of the playback position; do not confuse "watched to the end" with the persisted completion indicator ([RF-08](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais)). Distinguish, where applicable, the states not started, in progress, completed, unavailable and removed/missing, preserving the user's progress during a new scan ([RF-04, RF-05, RF-08](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais)). Coursera's public course page and Awwwards' course showcase are references only for course identity/context; they do not establish LearnVault's learning semantics.

### Player and focus session

Treat the player as a central study surface: offer clear play/pause controls, timeline seeking, volume and current/total time; keep the course/lesson context and the progress for resuming. The requirements establish playback, media delivery with seek support and a resume position ([RF-07–08, RNF-04](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais)); Plex and Infuse are media player references for inspection, not authorities for feature parity with LearnVault ([Plex](https://support.plex.tv/articles/200392226-plex-web-app-player/), [Infuse](https://firecore.com/infuse)). The focus/Pomodoro controls must be visually distinct from the playback controls and must not change the course completion semantics; keep the session state understandable and allow returning to the lesson navigation. Offer keyboard operation with visible focus and avoid shortcut conflicts ([WAI keyboard guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/), [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)).

### Settings and server login

Organize settings by user task and mode, instead of a single undifferentiated list. In offline desktop mode, make the selected library location and the explicit new-scan action clear; folder selection is native and the desktop does not require an account/login. In server mode, preserve the login/logout and account/credentials behavior; do not present server authentication controls as necessary for offline use ([RF-06, RF-09](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais)). Clearly distinguish the removal of media with a destructive appearance (an explicit persistent deletion marker) from a scan and explain that progress survives it ([RF-04–06](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais)).

### Desktop adaptation

Design for a resizable desktop window: keep a useful hierarchy between catalog and player at wide sizes, but recover horizontal space for media or content at narrower sizes; a hideable sidebar is a platform-compatible desktop pattern to evaluate ([Apple Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)). Use responsive reorganization instead of shrinking a dense multi-column desktop view indefinitely. Treat platform conventions as guidance for adaptation — not as a justification for introducing gestures exclusive to mobile devices or hidden actions available only on hover; cross-platform desktop support is a requirement ([RF-09](../product/requirements.md#requisitos-funcionais), [Windows app design](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/)).

### Accessibility

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline: keyboard operation and visible focus, sufficient text contrast and target size subject to the criterion's exceptions ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [focus visible](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html), [minimum contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [minimum target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum)). Use semantic labels and a predictable focus order; make sure focus is not hidden behind a persistent player/navigation interface. Respect the reduced-motion preference and keep state indications that do not depend on colour, since LearnVault explicitly requires reduced motion and screen-reader labels ([NFR-08](../product/requirements.md#non-functional-requirements)). The WAI-ARIA APG guides the keyboard behavior of widgets, but does not replace semantic controls nor WCAG conformance ([WAI keyboard interface](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/)).
