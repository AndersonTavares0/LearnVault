# Product UI reference board

Research notes for the LearnVault premium interface direction. Sources below are official public product or standards pages, reviewed using ordinary HTTP GET. These are references for interaction principles, not specifications: do not copy product assets, brand, proprietary copy, or pixel-level layouts. **Documented** describes claims or features on the cited page; **inference** is our design interpretation. No source assets were downloaded or reused.

| Product / source | Borrow | Avoid | Apply to LearnVault |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Readwise Reader** — [Readwise Reader](https://readwise.io/read) | **Documented:** reading and saved-material workspace. **Inference:** make the current learning action easy to resume and keep reading controls quiet. | Do not turn the course library into an imported-content/inbox product; originals remain read-only. | Course and lesson views should foreground “continue learning,” with secondary metadata subordinate. |
| **Things 3** — [Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/) | **Documented:** personal task manager centered on planning a day, managing projects, and making progress. **Inference:** restrained hierarchy and a clear next action keep dense organization approachable. | Avoid task-manager metaphors or excessive nested controls in a study library. | Use concise section headings, clear primary actions, and compact progress summaries on course cards. |
| **Linear** — [Features](https://linear.app/features) | **Documented:** product presents structured work tracking and workflows. **Inference:** consistent, compact visual language supports scanning across repeated items. | Avoid importing issue-tracker density, status colors everywhere, or speed-first shortcuts that obscure learning context. | Keep progress/status treatment consistent across library, course, and progress screens; use color as reinforcement, not sole encoding. |
| **Notion** — [Product](https://www.notion.so/product) | **Documented:** flexible knowledge/workspace product. **Inference:** generous whitespace and readable type help mixed-content pages feel legible. | Avoid blank-canvas ambiguity and unlimited configuration; LearnVault should provide a purposeful study structure. | Keep curriculum and notes content readable with deliberate spacing and clear content hierarchy. |
| **Apple Books** — [Apple Books](https://www.apple.com/apple-books/) | **Documented:** book-discovery and reading product. **Inference:** recognizable cover-shaped imagery helps people distinguish items at a glance. | Never copy covers or use imagery that implies a real title when unavailable. | Preserve a stable cover aspect ratio; provide a designed neutral fallback when no course thumbnail exists, with title text as an accessible fallback. |
| **Coursera** — [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/) | **Documented:** online learning catalog. **Inference:** course-oriented cards and visible learning context reduce ambiguity in a large catalog. | Avoid promotional marketplace clutter, fabricated ratings, or urgency cues. | Cards should prioritize course title, instructor/source when available, and honest completion/progress state. |
| **W3C WCAG 2.2** — [Understanding Success Criterion 1.4.11: Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html) | **Documented:** visual information needed to identify UI components and states must meet 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors (subject to criterion exceptions). | Do not rely on subtle low-contrast borders or color alone to communicate focus/progress. | Check controls, focus indicators, progress bars, and fallback-cover labels for sufficient contrast; pair color with text or shape where meaningful. |
| **W3C WCAG 2.2** — [Understanding Success Criterion 2.4.7: Focus Visible](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html) | **Documented:** keyboard-operable interfaces need a visible focus indicator. | Avoid removing browser focus outlines without an equally visible replacement. | Ensure links, card actions, menus, and player controls retain an obvious keyboard focus state. |

## State and fallback guidance

These are **design inferences**, not product claims from the sources:

- **Progress:** show a labeled, determinate progress indicator only when a meaningful denominator exists. Include a textual value (for example, completed lessons / total lessons or a percentage); distinguish not-started from unknown/unavailable rather than rendering both as zero.
- **Empty library:** explain the state plainly and offer the relevant next action (such as selecting or scanning an existing course folder); do not fabricate sample courses or imply content was lost.
- **Empty course/progress views:** explain why there is no activity yet and offer a route back to the course or next lesson. Avoid empty charts that suggest measured zero activity when data is absent.
- **Fallback covers:** use a consistent neutral visual system, preserve the same image dimensions and title placement as real covers, and allow long titles to wrap safely. Do not hotlink, reproduce, or synthesize branded third-party cover art. Ensure text remains legible and meaning does not depend on color alone.
- **Hierarchy:** prioritize the next learning action, then course identity and progress, with administrative metadata and controls visually secondary. Keep keyboard focus visible and provide text equivalents for progress and other non-text status cues.
